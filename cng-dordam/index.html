1. What the game collects

Nothing. Nothing is collected because nothing is sent anywhere. There is no server behind this game, no login, no online leaderboard and no advertising.

Specifically, the game never collects, requests or receives:

  • your name, email address, phone number or any other contact detail
  • your offers, your fares, your statistics, your streak or your play times
  • your location, at any precision
  • your device identifiers, Android ID or advertising ID
  • analytics, usage statistics, diagnostics or crash reports
  • your contacts, photos, files, camera or microphone

Every driver, every excuse, every destination and every fare calculation lives inside the app package on your phone. The daily challenge is not fetched from anywhere — it is derived on your device from today's date, which is why everyone in the world gets the same one without a server being involved. Nothing about your haggling is reported.

2. What is stored on your device

So that your progress survives closing the game, the following is saved in the app's own private storage — a memory-mapped key-value store under the app's private files/mmkv directory, which the Android sandbox makes unreadable to every other app on the phone:

What is savedDetail
Statistics Total taka saved, negotiations played, wins, times stranded, times you caved, refusals, cracks, bluffs won, best bluff streak, longest negotiation, biggest single save, which drivers you have met and how often, days played and your current streak
Your excuse collection Which of the driver's lines you have already heard, so the compendium can show what you have unlocked
Daily challenge record The date of the last daily you completed, so the streak counter and the "already played today" state work
Settings and small preferences Language, theme, volume, which sound channels are on (engine, street, voice), haptics, reduced motion, the district you last picked, your best race score, and whether you have seen the help screen

An in-progress negotiation is deliberately not saved — closing the game mid-haggle returns you to the home screen rather than to a half-finished argument.

This data belongs to you. It is readable only by this app, is never transmitted, and is removed when you erase your data in the game, clear the app's data from Android Settings, or uninstall the game.

3. Permissions

The released build declares exactly one real Android permission:

PermissionWhy it is needed
android.permission.VIBRATE Haptic feedback when you haggle, when the driver's price cracks and when a negotiation ends. You can turn haptics off in Settings. It is an install-time permission that grants access to the vibration motor and to no information whatsoever.

If you inspect the manifest of the released build you will also see com.skrchowdhury.cngdordam.DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION. That entry is generated automatically by an Android system library. It is signature-level — only a build signed with this app's own key could ever hold it — it is scoped to this app alone, and it grants access to nothing. It exists so the game's internal message receivers are not exposed to other apps, which is a privacy protection rather than a privacy cost. It is listed here only so that the count matches what you actually see.

There is no internet, storage, location, camera, microphone, contacts, phone or notification permission.

4. Network access

The game makes no network requests, and the released build cannot make one: without INTERNET, the operating system itself refuses. There is no networking code anywhere in the app — no HTTP client, no socket, no analytics beacon.

This is stronger than a promise, and it is deliberate. React Native's own manifest adds INTERNET because the development bundler needs it, so the release build explicitly removes it again before packaging. The published binary is incapable of opening a connection.

Confirming it takes ten seconds: turn on airplane mode and play. Everything works, including tomorrow's daily challenge.

5. Sound

The engine idle, the street ambience and the driver's grunts, laughs and refusals are a mix of two things: tones the app generates while it runs, and a small set of recorded sound files that ship inside the app package.

Those files are decoded on your device when the game starts. Because decoding them needs an audio decoder, the app bundles one — you will see media libraries such as libavformat.so if you unpack the build. It is used only to read the game's own bundled sound files. It reads nothing else, opens no files you own, and cannot reach the network.

The game has no microphone permission and records nothing. Sound only ever travels outward, to your speaker.

6. Libraries in the game

The game is built with React Native and bundles open-source libraries for rendering, animation, gestures, local storage, haptics, audio playback, the system share sheet and screenshot capture, along with a Bangla typeface and the game's own sound files.

There is no advertising network, analytics SDK, attribution SDK, crash reporter, A/B testing framework, push-notification service or social-media SDK in this game. No bundled library contacts a server at runtime; the released build has no permission that would let one.

7. Android Auto Backup

Android's Auto Backup is switched off for this game (android:allowBackup="false"). Your statistics, streak and excuse collection are therefore never copied to your Google account, never included in a phone-to-phone transfer, and exist in exactly one place: the phone you played on.

The honest trade-off is worth stating plainly. It means a new phone starts you from zero — there is nothing to restore. That is the cost of the guarantee, and for a game whose entire promise is that your data stays put, it is the right way round.

8. Sharing a result card

After a day's commute the game can draw your result as an image and hand it to Android's share sheet, together with a one-line message such as "CNG দরদাম — saved ৳340 today."

This only ever happens when you tap Share. The image is drawn on your device into a temporary file, and the share sheet is where you choose the destination — a chat, an email to yourself, your gallery, or nothing at all if you dismiss it. Nothing is uploaded automatically, nothing is sent if you cancel, and the developer never receives a copy or knows that you shared.

What happens to the image after you choose a destination is governed by whichever app or service you picked, not by this policy. The card shows your fares, savings and driver names for that run — it contains nothing about you personally, because the game holds nothing about you personally.

Handing a file to another app needs a file provider, so the manifest declares one (cl.json.RNShareFileProvider). It is not exported to other apps, and it grants read access to one temporary image at the moment you share it — never to your storage.

9. Links that leave the app

The about screen has two links to the developer's public profiles on GitHub and LinkedIn. Tapping one asks Android to open that address in your browser.

The game itself cannot load either page — it has no internet permission. It only hands the address to the operating system, exactly as tapping a link in a message would. From that point you are in your own browser, under the privacy policies of GitHub or LinkedIn and of that browser. No identifier, no game data and nothing else about you is attached to the address.

10. Retention and deletion

Data is kept on your device for as long as you keep it, and nowhere else. You are in full control of removing it:

  • Settings › Erase all data inside the game wipes your statistics, streak, excuse collection and preferences immediately and irreversibly
  • Settings › Apps › CNG দরদাম › Storage › Clear data removes everything at once
  • Uninstalling the game removes all of it

There is no deletion request to submit, because no copy exists off your device for anyone to delete. With Auto Backup disabled, there is not even a copy in your own Google account.

11. Security

Your data is protected by the Android application sandbox: the game's private storage is readable only by this app, and only on an unmodified device. There is no additional encryption layer, and none is claimed — on a rooted or compromised phone, the sandbox guarantee is weaker, and that is true of every app.

The strongest protection here is structural rather than cryptographic: data that is never transmitted cannot be intercepted, and a build with no network permission cannot transmit it.

12. Your rights

Data-protection laws such as the GDPR give you rights of access, correction, export and erasure over personal data a service holds about you. Those rights are fully satisfied here in the simplest possible way: no personal data is ever collected, so there is no profile to access, nothing to correct and nothing for anyone else to erase.

The equivalent controls live in the game itself and are always available to you — everything the game knows is on screen in the statistics and compendium views, and one button erases it.

  • Turn on airplane mode and play. Nothing stops working — not the daily challenge, not the excuse book, not the sound.
  • On the Play listing, About this app › App permissions › See more shows the full declared list.
  • With the Android SDK installed, aapt2 dump permissions app-release.apk prints the build's complete permission list — VIBRATE plus the signature-level entry described above, and nothing else.

14. Children

The game collects no data from anyone, children included. It shows no ads, contains no in-app purchases, has no chat, no messaging, no online leaderboard and no user-generated content.

The subject matter is an everyday argument about the price of a rickshaw ride. Nobody is harmed, no money is real, and the worst thing that happens is that a driver shrugs and drives off. There is no gambling: the walk-away bluff is a decision with an uncertain outcome, but nothing is wagered, nothing is purchased, no odds are shown, and the taka figure is a score rather than a balance. The only way the game interacts with anything outside itself is the share sheet, which you open yourself and which sends only the image you asked it to send.
