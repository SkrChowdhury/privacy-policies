The short version: Prostuti is a study planner for SSC, HSC, university admission, BCS and bank exams. Your syllabus progress, routine, study sessions, model-test scores and notes stay on your phone, and the daily plan is worked out on the phone. There is no account, no developer server and no analytics, and the developer receives nothing.

Three Google services are involved, and this page says exactly what each of them receives: Google AdMob, which shows banner ads in the free version (and receives your device's advertising ID); Google Play Billing, which sells the one-time Prostuti Pro; and Google Drive, but only if you turn on backup, which then keeps a copy of your study data in a hidden folder of your own Drive. The app also downloads the latest exam dates from a public Google Sheet; that request carries nothing about you.

1. What the developer collects

Nothing. There is no account, no developer server and no code in the app that sends your data to the developer.

In particular, the developer never collects, requests or receives:

  • your name, phone number or any contact detail (your Google account's email address, if you turn on Drive backup, stays on the phone; §6)
  • your exam, group, board or the date you set for it
  • your chapter statuses, revision schedule, routine or daily plan
  • your focus sessions, study time, streak or model-test scores
  • the notes you write on chapters you are stuck on
  • your location, at any precision
  • analytics, usage statistics, diagnostics or crash reports
  • your contacts, photos, files, camera or microphone

Two small items live outside MMKV: while a focus session has Do Not Disturb on, your previous Do Not Disturb mode is kept in the app's private SharedPreferences so it can be restored even after a crash; and Android's own schedulers (AlarmManager and WorkManager) hold the reminders and the nightly backup task the app has set. The progress-card picture (§12) is drawn into the app's cache when you share or save it.

None of this is transmitted, except the copy you choose to back up to your own Drive (§6). It is removed when you clear the app's data or uninstall it (§17).

3. How the plan is made

The daily plan, the revision dates, your pace, the countdowns and the statistics are all calculated on the phone from the syllabus built into the app and what you have marked. Nothing is looked up from a server, which is why Prostuti works in airplane mode.

4. Advertising

The free version shows Google AdMob banner ads in a strip at the bottom of the Syllabus, Chapters, Stats and Settings screens. There are no ads on Home, in the routine, the focus timer, the plan or during onboarding, and no pop-up, video or rewarded ads. Prostuti Pro removes every ad. Ads are limited to content rated for general audiences (G).

Consent first

Before any ad is requested, the app asks Google's User Messaging Platform whether consent is needed where you live (for example in the EEA or the UK). If it is, Google's consent form appears and ads load only after you answer. Where the form applies, Settings › Ad privacy options lets you change your choice later.

What Google receives

To select, serve and measure ads, Google may collect your device's advertising ID, device information such as model, Android version and language, an approximate location derived from your IP address, standard ad events such as whether an ad was shown or tapped, and the ads SDK's own diagnostics. This goes to Google, not to the developer, who sees only aggregate earnings in the AdMob dashboard. Google's description of how it uses data from apps that use its services is at policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

What Google does not receive

The ad request carries no data from inside the app — not your exam, your syllabus, your scores, your notes or your routine.

Turning ad personalisation off

In Settings › Google › All services › Ads, choose Delete advertising ID (on older versions, Opt out of Ads Personalisation). The app keeps working exactly as before.

5. Purchases

Prostuti is free, and every study feature is in the free version. Prostuti Pro is one optional one-time purchase (৳250 in Bangladesh) through Google Play Billing; there is no subscription. Pro removes ads and adds more exams at once, Drive backup, extra themes, monthly statistics, more routine templates and a weekly streak freeze.

Google handles the payment and your payment details; the developer never sees them. When the app starts it connects to Google Play to show the local price and to check whether your account owns Pro (so Pro returns after a reinstall and is removed after a refund), and it acknowledges a new purchase as Google requires. The purchase token Google returns is kept on the phone (§2).

6. Google Drive backup

Drive backup is part of Pro, and optional and off by default. Making a backup needs Pro; restoring one does not. When you tap ব্যাকআপ চালু করো (turn on backup) or ড্রাইভ থেকে ফিরিয়ে আনো (restore), you sign in with your Google account and the app asks for the single, non-sensitive drive.appdata permission: access to a hidden folder in your Drive that only Prostuti can see (Google's app data folder). It gives no access to the rest of your Drive.

The app keeps one file there, prostuti-backup.json, containing your settings, your exams, your syllabus edits and chapter statuses, the revision schedule, your routine, your focus sessions, chapters finished per day, your model-test scores and your notes, with the app version and the time it was made. It never contains your Pro status, the purchase token or your email address.

Backups happen when you tap এখনই ব্যাকআপ নাও (back up now) and, while প্রতি রাতে নিজে থেকে (nightly) is on, once a night around 2 a.m. when the phone is on Wi-Fi or charging, and after a focus session if the last backup is more than six hours old. Restoring shows the backup's date, exam and progress and asks before it replaces the study data on the phone.

The app remembers which Google account is connected (its email address, shown in the backup sheet) on the phone only. অ্যাকাউন্ট বদলাও (change account) signs out and forgets it. To delete the backup file, use Google Drive › Settings › Manage apps › Prostuti › Delete hidden app data; to revoke the app's access, visit myaccount.google.com/permissions. The file is held by Google under Google's privacy policy; the developer has no access to it.

7. Exam dates from a public sheet

Board and admission dates change, so the app checks a public Google Sheet kept by the developer for the latest exam dates, syllabus corrections and an occasional notice (for example, “the SSC routine is out”). It downloads the sheet's tabs as plain CSV from docs.google.com when the app starts and, at most, every six hours when you return to it. If the sheet can't be reached, the dates built into the app (or the last downloaded copy) are used.

This is a read-only download. The request carries nothing about you — no identifier, no account and no study data. Like any web server, Google sees the phone's IP address and the file asked for, under Google's privacy policy.

8. Reminders, focus sessions and Do Not Disturb

  • Reminders are optional. When you turn them on, Android asks for permission to show notifications, and the app schedules them on the phone: ten minutes before a routine block, a daily summary in the evening, revision due, the evening before an exam, the exam-day checklist and a streak reminder — at most three a day, never in quiet hours. Nothing is fetched or sent to produce them. After a restart the app re-schedules them.
  • Focus sessions show an ongoing notification with the time left and pause / stop buttons. If you pick a background sound (rain, café or fan — recordings inside the app), it keeps playing with the screen off as a media-playback service, only while the session runs.
  • Do Not Disturb is optional. If you switch it on and grant Android's Do Not Disturb access, the app switches the phone to priority-only while a focus session runs (calls and alarms still come through) and puts your previous mode back when the session is paused, stopped or finished.

9. Home-screen widgets

The three widgets — next exam, syllabus progress and today's plan — are drawn on the phone from the same on-device data. Ticking a plan row on the widget ticks it in the app. They use no network.

11. Network access

The syllabus, the plan, the routine, the timer, the statistics and the widgets all work without a connection. The app's own code makes only these requests:

  • The exam-date sheet on docs.google.com (§7) — carries nothing about you.
  • Drive backup on www.googleapis.com (Drive API v3) — only if you turn backup on or restore (§6).

The rest comes from Google's libraries: Google Mobile Ads and its consent form (§4), Google Play Billing (§5), Google Sign-In for Drive backup (§6) and Google Play's in-app review card (§13). Every connection uses HTTPS.

12. Sharing and saving the progress card

The Share screen draws a picture of your progress (streak, syllabus percentage, days left) on the phone. Share hands it to Android's share sheet, and where it goes is your choice. Save writes it to your gallery in an album called Prostuti. The app never reads your photos, and nothing is uploaded on its own.

13. Ratings and feedback

After you finish a focus session on a few different days, the app may show Google Play's own rating card. Google Play draws it and decides whether it appears; the app is not told whether you rated or what you chose. In Settings you can also rate the app: any rating can go to Google Play, and if you choose to send feedback instead, your email app opens with a message to the address below that you can read, edit or discard before sending.

14. Libraries in the app

The app is built with React Native and bundles open-source libraries for navigation, animation, storage (MMKV), notifications, home-screen widgets, background tasks (WorkManager), haptics, audio and sharing. None of them contacts a server.

The ones that use the network are Google Mobile Ads with the User Messaging Platform (§4), Google Play Billing (§5), Google Sign-In (§6) and Google Play In-App Review (§13). The ads SDK brings Google's measurement interface and its diagnostics transport; the app itself contains no Firebase or Google Analytics, crash reporter, attribution SDK or social-media SDK.

15. Android Auto Backup

Android's Auto Backup is switched off (android:allowBackup="false"), so nothing from the app is copied to your Google account automatically. Use Drive backup (§6) if you want a copy.

16. Links that leave the app

The app can open: its Google Play page, your email app (to write to the address below), this privacy policy, and the developer's website and LinkedIn. Each is handed to Android exactly as tapping a link in a message would, with nothing about you attached.

17. Retention and deletion

Everything the app saves stays on your device until you remove it:

  • editing a chapter, note, score or routine block in the app changes or deletes it at once
  • Settings › Apps › Prostuti › Storage › Clear data removes all of it
  • uninstalling the app removes all of it
  • a Drive backup, if you made one, is deleted from Google Drive › Settings › Manage apps › Prostuti › Delete hidden app data

There is no deletion request to send the developer, because no copy exists anywhere the developer can reach. Data held by Google for advertising, purchases or your Drive is governed by Google's own controls.

18. Security

Your data is protected by the Android application sandbox, and every connection the app makes uses HTTPS. The strongest protection is structural: your study data is never sent to the developer, so there is no server to breach. A Drive backup sits in your own Google account, protected by its sign-in.

19. Your rights

Data-protection laws such as the GDPR give you rights of access, correction, export and erasure over personal data a service holds about you. The developer holds none. Your data on the phone is yours to correct in the app, copy with Drive backup (§6) and erase (§17); data held by Google is managed through your Google account and the ad controls in §4.

20. Children

Prostuti is made for students aged 13 and over and is not directed at children under 13: it shows ads and offers a purchase. Ads are limited to content rated G, and the consent form decides where personalised ads are allowed. The app has no chat and no content shared between users, and the developer knowingly collects no data from anyone, children included.
