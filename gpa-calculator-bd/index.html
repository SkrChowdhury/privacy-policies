1. What the app collects

Nothing. Nothing is collected because nothing is sent anywhere. There is no server behind this app, no login, no profile and no advertising.

Specifically, the app never collects, requests or receives:

  • your name, email address, phone number or any other contact detail
  • your marks, grades, subjects, results or any other academic information
  • your institution, roll number, registration number or board
  • your location, at any precision
  • your device identifiers, Android ID or advertising ID
  • analytics, usage statistics, diagnostics or crash reports
  • your contacts, photos, files, camera or microphone

2. What is stored on your device

So that your work survives closing the app, the following is saved in the app's own private storage — a memory-mapped key-value store under the app's private files/mmkv directory, which the Android sandbox makes unreadable to every other app on the phone:

What is savedWhy
Subjects, marks, grades and credits you have entered on the SSC/HSC and university screens, including retake flags So a half-finished calculation is still there when you come back to it
Results you choose to save, each with a snapshot of the grading scale it was calculated on So reopening a saved result reproduces the exact figure, even if you later edit that scale
Grading scales you create or duplicate So a custom scale only has to be built once. Built-in scales ship inside the app and are not stored
Settings: theme, language, Bangla numerals, and whether you have seen the intro So the app opens the way you left it

This data belongs to you. It is readable only by this app, is never transmitted, and is removed when you delete it in the app, clear the app's data from Android Settings, or uninstall the app.

3. Permissions

The released build declares exactly one real Android permission:

PermissionWhy it is needed
android.permission.VIBRATE The short haptic tap you feel when a result lands. It is an install-time permission that grants access to the vibration motor and to no information whatsoever.

If you inspect the manifest of the released build you will also see com.skrchowdhury.cgcalculatorbd.DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION. That entry is generated automatically by an Android system library. It is signature-level — only a build signed with this app's own key could ever hold it — it is scoped to this app alone, and it grants access to nothing. It exists so the app's internal message receivers are not exposed to other apps, which is a privacy protection rather than a privacy cost. It is listed here only so that the count matches what you actually see.

There is no internet, storage, location, camera, microphone, contacts, phone or notification permission.

Permissions that were deliberately removed

Android libraries merge their own permission requests into the app that uses them, whether or not the app uses the feature those permissions are for. The file library used here ships a download manager, and would otherwise have added INTERNET, network and Wi-Fi state, WAKE_LOCK, external storage read/write and background-download permissions to the released app. None of that is used — only reading and writing a single file in the app's own cache directory — so those permissions are explicitly stripped from the release build.

The effect is that the declared permission surface of the shipped app matches what the app actually does, rather than what its dependencies theoretically could.

4. Network access

The app makes no network requests, and the released build cannot make one: without INTERNET, the operating system itself refuses. Every calculation, every grading scale and every saved result is computed and stored on your phone.

This is easy to confirm — put the phone in airplane mode and use the app normally. Nothing degrades, because nothing was ever coming from a server.

Internet access is declared only in the development build, so the bundler on a developer's own machine can serve code during development. It is not merged into the build published on Google Play.

5. Libraries in the app

The app is built with React Native and bundles open-source libraries for navigation, list rendering, animation, gestures, local storage, haptics, translation, image capture, the system share sheet and the system file picker.

There is no advertising network, analytics SDK, attribution SDK, crash reporter, A/B testing framework, push-notification service or social-media SDK in this app. No bundled library contacts a server at runtime; the released build has no permission that would let one.

6. Android Auto Backup

The app allows Android's built-in Auto Backup. That is an operating-system feature rather than part of this app: when it is enabled on your phone, Android may copy the app's saved data to your own Google account, so your history and custom scales can be restored if you set up a new phone, or transferred directly during a phone-to-phone setup.

What is included in that backup is exactly the app's key-value store — saved results, custom scales and settings. Nothing else is included, because there is nothing else.

The backup is held in your Google account under Google's privacy policy. The developer has no access to it and receives no copy of it. To turn it off, use Android's own setting — typically Settings › Google › Backup, or the per-app backup control your device provides.

7. Files you export or share

Three features can move data off your device, and every one of them requires you to act:

  1. Export backup. Writes a .json file containing your saved results and custom scales to the app's own cache, then hands it to Android's share sheet so you can put it wherever you want — Drive, Files, a chat, an email to yourself. The file is named for the day it was made.
  2. Share a result card. Renders an image of a result and hands that image to the share sheet.
  3. Share a grading scale. Hands a small .json file describing the scale to the share sheet, so another student can import it.

In all three cases you choose the destination, and the file goes only where you send it. What happens to it afterwards is governed by whichever app or service you chose, not by this policy. Nothing is uploaded automatically, and nothing is sent if you dismiss the share sheet.

Because no storage permission is requested, this all happens through Android's own share and document mechanisms — the app is granted access to the one file involved, not to your storage.

8. Reading files you pick

When restoring a backup or importing a scale, the app reads only the single file you select through the system document picker. It has no ability to browse your storage, list your files, or open anything you did not choose. If the file is not a valid backup, it is rejected and nothing is changed.

Importing merges into your library rather than overwriting it, and an imported scale is always added as a copy, so an import can never silently destroy work you already had.

9. Retention and deletion

Data is kept on your device for as long as you keep it, and nowhere else. You are in full control of removing it:

  • Clear on a calculator screen empties that screen's entries
  • Delete on a saved result or a custom scale removes that item
  • Settings › Apps › GPA Calculator BD › Storage › Clear data removes everything at once
  • Uninstalling the app removes all of it

There is no deletion request to submit, because no copy exists off your device for anyone to delete. If you have Auto Backup enabled, remember that a copy may also exist in your own Google account until you remove it there.

10. Security

Your data is protected by the Android application sandbox: the app's private storage is readable only by this app, and only on an unmodified device. There is no additional encryption layer, and none is claimed — on a rooted or compromised phone, the sandbox guarantee is weaker, and that is true of every app.

The strongest protection here is structural rather than cryptographic: data that is never transmitted cannot be intercepted, and a build with no network permission cannot transmit it.

11. Your rights

Data-protection laws such as the GDPR give you rights of access, correction, export and erasure over personal data a service holds about you. Those rights are fully satisfied here in the simplest possible way: no personal data is ever collected, so there is no profile to access, nothing to correct and nothing for anyone else to erase.

The equivalents live in the app itself and are always available to you — export gives you your data as a readable file, editing corrects it, and delete or uninstall erases it.

12. Verifying these claims

  • Open Settings › Apps › GPA Calculator BD › Permissions. Vibration is the only entry.
  • Turn on airplane mode and use every feature of the app. Nothing stops working.
  • With the Android SDK installed, aapt2 dump permissions app-release.apk prints the build's complete permission list — VIBRATE plus the signature-level entry described above, and nothing else.

13. Accuracy of results

The app implements the Bangladesh education board grading scale and the UGC uniform grading scheme, and lets you build your own scales for anything else. Institutions differ in how they calculate CGPA and in how they treat retakes, exclusions and the fourth subject, which is exactly why custom scales exist.

Results are for your own reference and planning. Your official result is whatever your education board or institution publishes — always treat the published marksheet or transcript as authoritative before relying on a number for anything that matters.

14. Children

The app is suitable for all ages and is used mainly by secondary and university students. Because it collects no data whatsoever, it collects nothing from children either. It shows no ads, contains no in-app purchases, has no chat and hosts no user-generated content.
