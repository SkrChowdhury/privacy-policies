The short version: the developer collects nothing about you. There is no server behind the game, no ads, no analytics and no crash reporting. Your tea stall — its money, stalls, workers and settings — is saved on your phone and nowhere the developer can reach.

Leaderboards and cloud save go through Google Play Games. From version 1.0.2, if you have a Google Play Games profile, the game can sign you in to Play Games, post your earnings and customers served to three Play Games leaderboards, and keep a copy of your game as a Play Games saved game in your own Google account. That goes to Google, not to the developer, and the game plays the same without it (§6).

Optional purchases go through Google Play. From version 1.0.1 the shop sells gem packs, a one-time shop-owner pack and a monthly মালিক পাস through Google Play Billing. Google handles the payment; the game only learns what you bought, keeps that on your phone and sends it nowhere. Everything in the game can still be earned by playing.

One small network request: about once an hour while you play, the game asks the free weather service Open-Meteo whether it is raining at a fixed point in Dhaka, so the street can rain when Dhaka does. The request contains those fixed coordinates and nothing about you or your phone. Without internet the game plays exactly the same, with the season's usual weather.

1. What the game collects

Nothing reaches the developer. There is no server behind this game, no Cha Dokan account and no advertising, so there is nowhere for anything to go. The one thing that leaves the phone with your game in it is optional and goes to Google: if you are signed in to Google Play Games, your scores and a cloud copy of your save, described in §6.

Specifically, the game never collects, requests or receives:

  • your name, email address, phone number or any other contact detail — the game has no place to type one (Play Games shows other players' gamer names on the leaderboard; it never gives the game an email address)
  • your play times or how often you play — and your progress and earnings reach no one but Google Play Games, and only while you are signed in
  • your location, at any precision — the weather is always Dhaka's, from fixed coordinates built into the app
  • your device identifiers, Android ID or advertising ID
  • analytics, usage statistics, diagnostics or crash reports
  • your contacts, photos, files, camera or microphone

Every customer, order, price, story and festival lives inside the app package on your phone. Nothing about the game is fetched, and nothing about your play is reported to the developer.

2. What is stored on your device

So that your stall is still there when you come back, the game saves to the app's own private storage — a memory-mapped key-value store (MMKV, id chadokan) under the app's private files/mmkv directory, which the Android sandbox makes unreadable to every other app on the phone:

What is savedDetail
The game Your money and gems, stall and recipe levels, which stalls are open, hired workers, decorations and stall colours, customers served, regulars met and stories heard, adda votes, achievements, album memories, festival rewards, today's tasks and rush-hour results, and the time of the last save — used to work out what the stall earned while you were away
Settings Sound, music, vibration, text size, whether notifications are on, whether the game has already asked about them, and which tips you have already seen
Weather The last reading from Open-Meteo: whether it was raining in Dhaka, and when that was checked
Purchases Whether you own the shop-owner pack and whether the মালিক পাস is running (both re-checked with Google Play each time the game opens), the purchase tokens of the last gem packs credited (so a pack is never counted twice), and the date the pass last gave its daily gems. No payment details: those stay with Google
Play Games bookkeeping Which Play Games achievements were reported, your latest place on the weekly leaderboard (for its weekly reward), when the game last asked Google Play for a rating, when the game last uploaded its cloud save to Play Games, this week's earnings for the weekly leaderboard, and the last leaderboard pages Play Games showed (the top players' gamer names and scores, and yours), so the leaderboard opens at once (see §6)

This data is readable only by this app, and it is removed when you clear the app's data in Android Settings or uninstall the game. Uninstalling deletes all of it from the phone; the only other copies are in your own Google account — Android's backup (§9) and, if you signed in to Play Games, the cloud save (§6).

4. Network access

The game itself makes one kind of request, and only while it is open: when it starts and then about once an hour, it asks Open-Meteo for the current rain at latitude 23.81, longitude 90.41 — a fixed point in Dhaka, the same for every player:

api.open-meteo.com/v1/forecast?latitude=23.81&longitude=90.41&current=precipitation,weather_code

The request carries no identifier, no key, no account and nothing about your progress. As with any web request, Open-Meteo's servers see the connection's IP address; see Open-Meteo's privacy terms. The answer — raining or not — is kept for an hour (see §2). If the request fails, or the phone is offline, the street simply uses the season's usual chance of rain.

The About page has two links, to the developer's LinkedIn profile and website. They open in your browser only when you tap them, and the game sends nothing with them.

Sharing. From version 1.0.5, the share page's button opens Android's share sheet with a picture the game draws on the phone — the game's name and icon, your day, your total earnings and how many stalls are open — and a line of text with the game's Play Store link. The picture is written to the app's own cache folder only so the app you pick can read it; nothing is sent unless you choose an app and send it. The copy button puts the same text on your clipboard.

Ratings. From version 1.0.5, at a happy moment (a new stall, a regular's whole story) the game may ask Google Play to show its rating sheet — from the third day, at most once every 30 days. Google Play decides whether it shows, and never tells the game whether or how you rated. The rating page's Play Store-এ রিভিউ লিখুন button opens the game's Play Store page.

Everything else goes to Google through the Google Play apps on the phone: purchases (§5) and, if you are signed in to Play Games, the leaderboards and the cloud save (§6). There is no telemetry channel and nothing is sent to the developer. Put the phone in airplane mode and play: everything works, including the money your stall earns while you are away; the leaderboards and the cloud save simply wait.

5. Purchases

The shop's gem packs, the one-time দোকান মালিক প্যাক and the monthly মালিক পাস are sold through Google Play Billing. Prices are shown as Google Play gives them. When you buy, Google Play's own sheet takes the payment; the game never sees your card, mobile-wallet or account details.

The game talks to Google Play — the Play Store app on your phone — when it opens, when it comes back from the background (to see what you own, so a refunded pack or an ended pass is taken back) and when you buy. Google Play tells it which product was bought and gives a purchase token; the game keeps that on the phone (see §2) and sends it to no server. Purchases are checked on the phone; there is no developer server.

The মালিক পাস renews every month until you cancel it in Google Play (Play Store › Payments & subscriptions › Subscriptions). What Google Play records about your purchases is held under Google's privacy policy.

6. Google Play Games: leaderboards and cloud save

Play Games is optional; the whole game works without it. If you have a Google Play Games profile, Play Games may sign you in automatically when the game opens. If you are not signed in, the game asks Play Games to show its own sign-in sheet only when you open the leaderboard. While you are signed in:

  • Your profile. Google gives the game your Play Games player ID and gamer name, so the leaderboard can show your row. No email address and no contacts.
  • Leaderboards. The game posts three numbers to Play Games leaderboards: total earnings (in thousands of taka), this week's earnings (the Dhaka week, Saturday to Friday) and happy customers served. It reads the gamer names and scores at the top of each board to draw the leaderboard screen, and keeps the last pages on the phone so the screen opens at once (refreshed when you open it, and in the background after you sign in or come back to the game). Who can see your scores depends on your Play Games profile's visibility, which you set in Play Games. Scores are posted when you leave the game, when you open the leaderboard, and when the leaderboards refresh in the background.
  • Cloud save. The save described in §2 — including whether you own the shop-owner pack or the মালিক পাস and the recent gem-pack purchase tokens — is stored as a Play Games saved game in your own Google account, at most every ten minutes when you leave the game and right after a purchase. On a new phone, if the cloud save has come further than the game on the phone, the game asks before it uses it.
  • Achievements. From version 1.0.5, 22 Play Games achievements — the in-game achievements, opening each stall, hearing each regular's whole story, the whole street and selling the street — are unlocked in your Play Games profile when your save earns them. The save remembers which ones were reported (§2).

Play Games data is held by Google under the Google Privacy Policy; the developer has no access to it beyond the public leaderboards every player sees. To stop it, sign out of Play Games or don't sign in. You can delete the game's scores and saved game from your Play Games profile or your Google Account.

7. Notifications

After your first couple of days at the stall, the game asks — in its own words, inside the game — whether you would like a note when something is happening at your stall. Only if you say yes does Android show its permission prompt.

Notifications are made on your phone: when you leave the game it schedules at most one, chooses its text from the game's own list, and keeps it out of the night hours (22:00 to 08:00). There is no push service and no server. You can turn them off in the game's Settings, or in Android's settings for the app, at any time.

8. Libraries in the game

The game is built with React Native and bundles open-source libraries for drawing, animation, gestures, navigation, local storage (MMKV), sound, vibration, local notifications (Notifee, with AndroidX WorkManager) and prayer-time calculation (Dhaka's sunset and prayer times for the street's day and Ramadan's iftar, worked out on the phone from fixed coordinates), along with Bangla typefaces.

It also bundles Google's Play In-App Review library, which from version 1.0.5 asks for a rating as described in §4.

From version 1.0.1 it includes Google Play Billing 9.1.0, through the open-source react-native-iap (openiap) library, for the purchases in §5. openiap also brings Google's Play Integrity client, which the game never calls.

From version 1.0.2 it includes Google Play Games Services (v2, 22.1.0) for the leaderboards and cloud save in §6.

There is no advertising network, analytics SDK, attribution SDK, crash reporter, A/B testing framework, push-notification service or social-media SDK in this game. Apart from the weather request in §4, Google Play Billing in §5 and Google Play Games in §6, no bundled library contacts a server.

9. Android Auto Backup

The game allows Android's built-in Auto Backup. That is an operating-system feature rather than part of this app: when it is on for your phone, Android may copy the game's save to your own Google account, so your stall comes back on a new phone or after reinstalling, or moves across during a phone-to-phone transfer.

What is included is exactly the game's storage described in §2 — nothing else from the app, no caches and no notification schedule.

The backup is held in your Google account under Google's privacy policy. The developer has no access to it and receives no copy of it. To turn it off, use Android's own setting — typically Settings › Google › Backup.

10. Retention and deletion

Data is kept on your device for as long as you keep it. You are in full control of it:

  • Settings › Apps › চা দোকান › Storage › Clear data removes everything on the phone at once
  • Uninstalling the game removes all of it from the phone
  • An Android backup in your Google account is managed — and can be deleted — from your Google account's backup settings
  • Play Games scores and the cloud save are deleted from your Play Games profile (the game's entry there) or your Google Account

There is no deletion request to submit to the developer, because no copy exists anywhere the developer can reach.

11. Security

On the device, your data is protected by the Android application sandbox: the game's private storage is readable only by this app, and only on an unmodified device. There is no additional encryption layer, and none is claimed. The weather request uses HTTPS; Play Games and Play Billing use Google's own encrypted connections.

The strongest protection here is structural: there is no server holding your data, so there is no breach of the developer's that could expose it.

12. Your rights

Data-protection laws such as the GDPR give you rights of access, correction, export and erasure over personal data a service holds about you. Those rights are satisfied here in the simplest possible way: the developer collects no personal data, so there is no profile to access, correct or erase. What Google Play Games and Google Play hold about you is yours to see and delete in your Google Account, under Google's privacy policy.

13. Verifying these claims

  • Open Settings › Apps › চা দোকান › Permissions. Notifications is the only one shown, and it is off until you agree to it; the others are install-time and are not shown as toggles.
  • Turn on airplane mode and play. Nothing stops working; the leaderboard shows a sample board, marked as one, until it can reach Play Games again.
  • With the Android SDK installed, aapt2 dump permissions app-release.apk prints the build's complete permission list — the ten in §3 plus the signature-level entry described there, and nothing else.

14. Children

চা দোকান is made for players aged 13 and up; it is not designed for children and is not part of Google Play's Families programme. It collects no data from anyone, children included: it shows no ads and has no chat, no messaging and no user-generated content. Its optional leaderboards and cloud save go through Google Play Games, and its optional purchases through Google Play, whose own controls (such as Play Games profile visibility and requiring authentication for purchases) apply.

15. Future versions

A later version may add optional ads (Google AdMob). They are not in this version. Before a version with them ships, this page will be updated to say exactly what they send, to whom, and how to switch them off — and the Google Play Data safety declaration with it.
