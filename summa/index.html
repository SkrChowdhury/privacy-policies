The short version: the developer collects nothing about you and never sees what you calculate. There is no server behind Summa, no account, no analytics, no crash reporting and no ads. Every calculation, tool and widget works on your phone, offline.

Two things involve Google, and each is limited to what it needs. Google Play handles the optional Summa Plus purchase — the app asks Google Play for its price and whether you own it. If you have Plus and turn on Drive backup, your history and settings are copied to a hidden folder in your own Google Drive. None of it is ever sent to the developer.

1. What the developer collects

Nothing. There is no server behind Summa and no account of the developer's. The developer never receives:

  • your calculations, results, history, notes, variables or memory
  • anything you type into the tools (unit converter, tax and discount, loan, dates, programmer, statistics, solver or graphing)
  • your name, email address, phone number or any other contact detail
  • your location, at any precision
  • your device identifiers, Android ID or advertising ID
  • analytics, usage statistics, diagnostics or crash reports
  • your contacts, photos, files, camera or microphone

Every calculation is worked out on your phone. The Google services described in §3 and §4 talk to Google, not to the developer.

2. What is stored on your device

So your work is there next time, the following is saved in the app's private storage (key-value files under the app's private files/mmkv directory, which the Android sandbox makes unreadable to every other app):

What is savedDetail
HistoryEach calculation's expression and result, when you made it, whether you pinned it, and any note you add. Saved only while Settings › Save history is on
Calculator stateThe memory value, your named variables and the last answer
SettingsTheme, accent colour, larger keys, digit grouping, amount in words, degrees or radians, save history, vibrate on key press, the expanded scientific keys and the last calculator mode
Summa PlusA cached copy of whether you own Plus (with Google Play's purchase token), so it works offline, and how many free tries of each Plus tool you have used. Re-checked with Google Play at startup
Drive backup stateWhether backup is on, when it last ran, and the ID of the backup file in your Drive. Never your password or email address
Calculator widgetThe sum currently on the 4 × 4 widget, in the widget's own private state file

Values typed into the tools are not saved. All of the above is removed when you clear the app's data from Android Settings or uninstall the app.

3. Summa Plus purchase

Summa is free. Summa Plus is an optional one-time purchase through Google Play Billing; there are no subscriptions. Google handles the payment and your payment details; the developer never sees them.

When the app starts, it asks Google Play for the price of Summa Plus in your currency and whether you already own it, so a refund removes Plus and a reinstall can restore it. When you buy, the app confirms the purchase with Google Play as Google requires. Restore purchase asks Google Play the same question again. These requests carry nothing from your calculations.

4. Google Drive backup

Drive backup is part of Summa Plus, and optional and off by default. When you turn it on, you sign in with your Google account and the app asks for the single, non-sensitive drive.appdata permission: access to a hidden folder in your Drive that only Summa can see (Google's app data folder). It gives no access to the rest of your Drive.

The app keeps one file there, summa-backup.json, containing your settings, your history (including notes), your variables and the memory value, with the time it was made. It never contains your Summa Plus status, the purchase token, your email address or any identifier. A backup runs at most once a day, and when you leave the app if anything has changed.

When you turn backup on and a backup already exists, the app asks whether to restore it. When you turn backup off, it stops, offers to delete the file from your Drive, and signs out. You can also remove it in Google Drive › Settings › Manage apps, and revoke the app's access at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Summa does not store your email address; Google's sign-in library on the phone remembers which account is signed in until backup is turned off. The file is held by Google under Google's privacy policy; the developer has no access to it.

5. Home-screen widgets and Quick Settings tile

  • The 4 × 4 calculator widget adds, subtracts, multiplies and divides on the phone. It keeps the current sum in its own private state, and follows the app's theme and accent.
  • The Last result widget shows the last answer from the app. Tapping it opens Summa with that number loaded, through a link that stays on the phone.
  • The Quick Settings tile only opens the app.

None of them uses the network.

7. Network access

Summa works fully offline. It goes online only for:

  • Google Play — the price of Summa Plus, whether you own it, and the purchase itself
  • Google sign-in and the Google Drive API (www.googleapis.com) — only while Drive backup is on

There is no developer server and no telemetry channel. Fonts and everything else the app shows are built in, so nothing is downloaded to display it.

8. Libraries in the app

Summa is built with React Native and bundles open-source libraries for drawing, animation, gestures, navigation, lists, local storage, haptics, the clipboard and the date picker, plus Google's Play Billing (9.1.0), Google Sign-In and Jetpack Glance libraries. There is no advertising network, analytics SDK, attribution SDK, crash reporter or social-media SDK.

Google's libraries bring a few components of their own. Play Billing includes Google's billing diagnostics (datatransport), active only while billing is connected. The purchase library also bundles the Play Integrity client, which the app never calls. Play Billing also includes Google's location library, but the app declares no location permission, so it cannot read your location.

9. Android Auto Backup

Disabled. Summa sets android:allowBackup="false", so Android does not copy its data to your Google account. The only backup is the optional Drive backup in §4, which you control.

10. Copying, exporting and links

  • Copy puts a result on the clipboard, only when you tap it.
  • Copy history as CSV, in History, puts your history on the clipboard as CSV text and offers Share, which hands it to Android's share sheet — sent wherever you choose. Nothing is uploaded by the app.
  • The developer's portfolio and LinkedIn links open in your browser, only when you tap them.

11. Retention and deletion

  • History: Delete from history for one entry or Clear all for everything, in History; or turn off Save history in Settings
  • Settings › Apps › Summa › Storage › Clear data, or uninstalling, removes everything on the phone
  • Drive backup is deleted as described in §4
  • Purchase records are kept by Google Play

There is no deletion request to send the developer, because the developer holds no copy of your data.

12. Security

On the phone, your data is protected by the Android app sandbox. Everything that leaves the phone goes over HTTPS to Google's own services. The strongest protection is structural: there is no developer server holding your data, so there is nothing of the developer's to breach.

13. Your rights

Data-protection laws such as the GDPR give you rights of access, correction, export and erasure over personal data a service holds about you. The developer holds none. Your data on the phone is yours to export (§10) and erase (§11), and the Drive backup lives in your own Google account.

14. Children

Summa is a general-purpose calculator, suitable for everyone but not designed specifically for children. It shows no ads, has no chat and no user-generated content, and collects no data from anyone, including children.

15. Changes to this policy
